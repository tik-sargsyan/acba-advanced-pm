Curriculum

The 10 sessions

Four blocks. Sessions 1 to 3 build the operator foundations. Sessions 4 to 6 run the product lifecycle with AI. Sessions 7 to 9 build the advanced systems: agents, multi-agent workflows and quality judges. Session 10 ships.

Week 0: assessment and setup sprint

Before session 1 you complete a short AI-experience assessment and a guided setup checklist: Claude, Claude Code, VS Code and a GitHub account. Tables are paired by experience level, so nobody waits and nobody drowns. You arrive at session 1 ready to build.

Sessions 1 to 3

Operator foundations

Session 1

How AI really works, and where it breaks

  • Tokens, context windows, hallucination, and why fluent is not true
  • Brilliant then useless: finding the jagged edge for your own tasks
  • Your data red lines: what never goes into a prompt, and the one question to ask before you paste
  • Live build: your workspace running, and your first bounded prompt with a checked source
  • You leave with: a working setup and an AI work card you can defend
Session 2

Your workspace and context engineering

  • Claude Code as an operator: it reads and writes real files inside one folder you chose
  • CLAUDE.md: standing rules that load before you type, and why the narrowest file wins
  • The permission gate: what actually says no, sorted by what it costs to undo
  • Live build: your rules file, your project folder, and your first automation
  • You leave with: an AI workspace configured around your real job
Session 3

Skills: package your expertise

  • Turn work you repeat every week into a skill Claude runs on demand
  • Skill design: instructions, worked examples, and the guardrails that hold output to a standard
  • Why the one-line description is the whole matching surface
  • Live build: a PRD writer and a decision-brief skill, from zero
  • You leave with: two working skills you use the next morning
Sessions 4 to 6

The product lifecycle, with AI

Session 4

Research to an evidence-backed spec

  • Synthesis that does not invent: it smooths, it fills gaps, and it sounds certain doing it
  • Cite or flag: every problem claim maps to a source, or it is marked as an assumption
  • Build a persona, then attack it with the evidence you actually have
  • Live build: an opportunity map and a one-page PRD with acceptance criteria and a negative flow
  • You leave with: a spec where a reviewer can trace every claim
Session 5

Data to a decision, without SQL

  • Three ways to get data to AI, and why read-only is the rule you own
  • The silent wrong number: state the population, denominator, period and filters, every time
  • Significance and confidence in plain language, and the traps AI falls into
  • Live build: a verified decision brief, with one number reproduced by hand
  • You leave with: a defensible call, and an alternative explanation you tested
Session 6

Communicating and presenting

  • One truth, three audiences: what an executive, a peer and an engineer each need first
  • Lead with the answer, then the evidence, then the ask
  • Strip the generic AI tone and keep your own voice
  • Live build: an executive update and a one-pager that shares as a link and prints clean
  • You leave with: a shareable one-pager and a clean PDF of your own work
Sessions 7 to 9

Advanced AI systems

Session 7

Agents and subagents

  • An agent is a loop, not an answer. The first question is what makes it stop.
  • Roles, boundaries and failure modes: what to delegate and what to keep
  • Subagents: fan the reading out, keep the reviewable thread short
  • Live build: a reviewer agent that checks your own documents before your stakeholders do
  • You leave with: a working agent and a stated boundary for what it may touch
Session 8

Multi-agent systems, MCP and the security line

  • Orchestration patterns: pipeline, fan-out, verifier, synthesis, and where each one pays
  • MCP and connectors: how AI reaches real systems, and why least privilege is a product decision
  • Prompt injection: anything the system reads in is untrusted input, including a customer message
  • Live build: a three-agent pipeline, research to draft to critique, with a planted injection
  • You leave with: a multi-agent workflow and a permission model you can take to risk
Session 9

AI features and the judge

  • Spec an AI feature in five fields: task, inputs, boundary, owner, fallback
  • Quality against cost against latency, and the honest case for not using AI
  • Score it, do not dictate it: rubrics, an AI judge, and seeded bad outputs that prove it works
  • Live build: a judge for your own feature, with a planted failure it has to catch
  • You leave with: an evaluated proposal with measured results, not claimed ones
Session 10

Capstone: build, ship, demoStudio

  • Scope like a knife: one user, one task, one magic moment
  • Build the core with mock data, then the QA pass and the guardrails
  • Live build: your prototype, running, with its empty and error states
  • Demo to the room in four beats, then the release decision: what ships, what waits, what it cannot do
  • You leave with: a working product and a release pack with the limits written down

The capstone thread

You pick your product idea in session 1 and shape it through the homework. The scope locks at session 5. From there every artifact feeds the finish: the PRD from session 4 defines it, the decision brief from session 5 justifies it, the one-pager from session 6 sells it, the agent and the judge from sessions 7 to 9 run inside it, and session 10 assembles and ships it. You graduate with a product you built, a system that runs it, and a release pack you could put in front of a risk committee.
